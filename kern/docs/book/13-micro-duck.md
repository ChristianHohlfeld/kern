# 13 The Micro Duck

The Micro Duck is the small bipedal walking robot from Pollen Robotics: about 25 cm tall, weighing
0.74 kg, with 14 Dynamixel XL330 servos in the legs and neck plus a fifteenth servo for the lower
beak. Pollen trains its motions as neural networks in MuJoCo (repository `microduck_rl`, framework
mjlab, learning algorithm rsl_rl) and exports them as ONNX files that the real robot loads.

Kern simulates this robot on Box3D and runs exactly these networks. The goal is fidelity: the same
model, the same servos, the same control rate, the same observation and the same action as in
training and on the robot. On top of that, Kern adds three things of its own:

- the **Lehrer** ("teacher"), Kern's own training system in C++ ([Chapter 14](14-lehrer.md)),
- the **jump** (`policies/jump.onnx`), the first motion of its own,
- the **play layer** (`policies/play.onnx`), a learned network that sits above Pollen's motions and
  decides what the duck does with a ball or a sock.

This chapter describes the duck at runtime: model, servo, timing, networks, sensors, brain, play
layer and controls. How to train networks yourself is covered in
[Chapter 15](15-your-own-models.md); how the simulation is matched to the real robot, in
[Chapter 16](16-calibration.md).

## 13.2 Principle: do not bypass the physics

A trained network only works if the simulation computes the same physics as the training. Kern
therefore applies fixed rules to the duck:

- No helper forces, no prescribed velocities, no trunk lock, no teleporting, no blending between
  networks. The duck stands by its servos and contacts alone.
- A servo consists only of Box3D's own joint features: the implicitly solved joint spring and a
  motor with target speed 0 for friction (Section 13.4).
- Fixed time step, counted in steps rather than wall-clock time. Identical inputs produce a gait
  that is identical bit for bit on all platforms checked in CI (determinism, [Chapter 14](14-lehrer.md#147-determinism)).

The physics itself (Box3D, contacts, joints) is described in [Chapter 6](06-physics.md).

## 13.3 The model

### Import from the MJCF

Pollen trains on `robot_allcollisions_backlash.xml` (15 bodies, 14 joints). The tool
`tools/micro_duck_import.py` reads this file and `velstand.onnx` and generates
`src/game/micro_duck_model.inc` and `src/game/micro_duck_velstand.inc` from them, and from Pollen's
STL files the simplified meshes (`third_party/microduck/micro_duck_meshes.inc`) and the colliders'
convex hulls:

```
python3 -I tools/micro_duck_import.py kern/third_party/microduck/robot_allcollisions_backlash.xml \
    <microduck_rl>/src/mjlab_microduck/robot/microduck/assets kern/third_party/microduck/velstand.onnx kern/src/game \
    kern/third_party/microduck/micro_duck_meshes.inc
```

The import checks joint names and order, the home pose against `default_joint_pos`, the order of
the observation, `action_scale` and the graph structure of the network (Sub, Div, 4× Gemm, ELU). If
anything differs, it aborts.

For each link, `kDuckLinks` (struct `DuckLinkDef`) contains: MJCF body, joint name, parent link,
joint position and axis in the home pose, joint limits, home angle, mass, centre of mass and the
full inertia tensor about the centre of mass. Kern builds the duck directly in the home pose (STAND
keyframe) and sets `JointComponent::reference` to the home angle so that every joint reads exactly
the MJCF angle.

### Masses and armature

Mass, centre of mass and inertia tensor come per body from the MJCF and are set with
`set_mass_data`. The trunk weighs 0.199 kg, the head 0.155 kg; 0.74 kg in total. Mass and inertia of
the lower beak are derived from the meshes (PLA 1.24 g/cm³, TPU 1.21 g/cm³) and subtracted from the
head.

MuJoCo adds the servo's rotor inertia (armature, 0.0018 kg·m² per joint) onto the joint axis in
reduced coordinates. Box3D works in maximal coordinates and has no gear joint. Kern therefore gives
the armature to the link as a mass property (`add_armature`):

```cpp
// a/2 (E + axis axis^T): 'armature' about the axis, half of it across
const f32 add[6] = {1 + ax.x * ax.x, 1 + ax.y * ax.y, 1 + ax.z * ax.z, ax.x * ax.y, ax.x * ax.z, ax.y * ax.z};
for (int k = 0; k < 6; ++k) inertia[k] += 0.5f * armature * add[k];
```

This is a thin disc on the joint axis and therefore a valid rigid body. A purely axial tensor would
not be one, and Box3D would gain energy from it. The difference from MuJoCo: the real rotor spins at
288:1 relative to the housing, whereas Kern's disc turns with the link. If the parent link rotates
about the same axis, the inertia is therefore slightly too large. Measured, the duck walks and turns
closer to training with armature and jitters considerably less (Section 13.14).

### Collision

What collides is, as in MuJoCo, a convex hull each – in Kern the hulls of the colliding parts from the import (`src/game/micro_duck_model.inc`). They make the duck touch the world exactly as in training:

| Part | Friction | Surface |
|---|---|---|
| Soles | 1 | – (collider with `builtin:plastic_step` as impact sound) |
| XL330 housings | 0 (as in training) | – |
| Beak pads | 1 | `tpu` |
| Shells of trunk and head (`trunk_base`, `jaw_soft`) | 0.5 | – |

The shells enclose the servos of the neighbouring links and never touch the duck itself (Kern sets
`no_collision` joints for that). A fallen duck lies on them and does not slide as if on ice.

Since the rework of the play layer, the physics knows **surface materials with their own pair
friction** (`physics::Surface`: `cloth`, `tpu`, `floor`). Two materials do not simply blend: TPU on
cotton has its own measured value, not the mean of TPU on TPU and cotton on cotton. `physics::surface_friction` returns a dedicated
value for certain pairs; all other pairs blend as in Box3D, as the geometric mean √(a·b):

| Pair | Friction | Justification in the code |
|---|---|---|
| Cotton knit on cotton knit (`cloth`/`cloth`) | 0.5 | yarn on yarn 0.3, knit on itself 0.4–0.6 |
| TPU on cotton (`tpu`/`cloth`) | 0.8 | elastomers on textile 0.6–1.0, the softer the higher |
| Cotton on wood or laminate floor (`cloth`/`floor`) | 0.4 | fabric on wood 0.3–0.5 |

The surface is carried by a collider (`ColliderComponent::surface`), a mesh (`MeshComponent::surface`)
or the world's ground (`WorldSettings::ground_surface`, `groundSurface` in the level JSON). In the
Micro Duck level and in the training scenes the ground is `floor`.

Box3D's contact slop is 5 mm; in the test `micro_duck_stands_on_the_floor` the soles sit 0.3–0.6 mm
into the ground.

### Scale 4

Box3D is designed for bodies from 0.1 to 10 m, but the duck's links are 2 to 4 cm in size. Kern
therefore builds it four times larger (`kWalkerScale = 4`) and converts in exactly one place
(`scaled()`), following Froude similarity:

| Quantity | Factor | at S = 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Length | S | 4 |
| Mass | S³ | 64 |
| Inertia | S⁵ | 1024 |
| Torque, servo stiffness | S⁴ | 256 |
| Damping | S⁴·⁵ | 512 |
| Time | √S | 2 |
| Velocity | √S | 2 |
| Angular velocity | 1/√S | 1/2 |

The policy always sees real units: Kern multiplies angular velocities by √4 = 2 before they go into
the network. All API values for the Lehrer, logic and sensors are likewise in robot units (real
metres, real seconds) unless stated otherwise.

## 13.4 The servo model

Pollen trains with the BAM model "m6" of the XL330-M288 at 7.4 V. Kern reproduces it as a **device
model**: a small law in real units on top of Box3D, whose parameters are kept in a profile
(`ServoProfile`).

| Parameter | Value (`xl330_profile()`) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `stiffness` K | 0.554 N·m/rad | V · kt/R · 0.5755 |
| `damping` D | 0.053 N·m·s/rad | back-EMF kt²/R plus viscous friction |
| `friction` F | 0.0048 N·m | Coulomb friction in the joint |
| `torque` T | 0.963 N·m | torque at full drive V · kt/R |
| `armature` A | 0.0018 kg·m² | rotor inertia through the gearbox |
| `delay` | 20 ms | command → servo |
| `spread_armature`, `spread_friction` | ±10 % | spread in training |
| `delay_min` … `delay_max` | 15 … 30 ms | latency range in training |

The law:

```
torque = clamp(K · (target − angle), −T, T) − D · velocity
+ Coulomb friction F
```

A single function implements it in every physics step for every servo joint, identically in the
Studio, in the Lehrer and on the calibration test bench (`game::servo_step`, called from
`GameWorld::drive_servos`):

```cpp
const f32 q = world.joint_angle(joint);
const f32 k = max_of(1e-6f, stiffness);
const f32 reach = torque > 0 ? torque / k : 1e9f;
world.set_joint_spring(joint, q + clamp(target - q, -reach, reach), k, max_of(0.0f, damping));
world.set_joint_motor(joint, friction > 0, 0, max_of(0.0f, friction));
```

- **Spring:** Box3D's joint spring pulls towards `angle + clamp(target − angle, ±T/K)`. The spring
  is solved implicitly and is stable at any stiffness. `set_joint_spring` converts N·m/rad, using the
  joint's axial effective mass, into Box3D's hertz and damping ratio – the same way Box3D computes
  its spring internally.
- **Saturation:** limiting the error to T/K yields the holding torque, just like the Dynamixel's
  saturating PWM. At K = 0.554 N·m/rad full torque is only reached at a deviation of 1.7 rad; the
  servos are soft.
- **Friction:** a motor with target speed 0 whose maximum torque is the friction.
- **Current limit:** with the firmware's 1.75 A limit (0.64 N·m) the duck can no longer get up from
  its back. Pollen explicitly trains without this limit (`scripts/infer_policy.py`); Kern follows
  suit.

`set_micro_duck_servos(level, trunk, profile)` gives a profile to all joints of a duck (with the
armature on every link and the bus delay); `set_micro_duck_servo(level, trunk, link, profile)` to a
single one (link 1–14 in policy order, 15 is the mouth). Profiles as JSON and their calibration are
described in [Chapter 16](16-calibration.md).

## 13.5 Timing and decimation

| | Training (MuJoCo) | Kern |
|---|---|---|
| Physics step | 5 ms (200 Hz) | 10 ms world time (`kWalkerStepHz` = 100 Hz), 16 sub-steps (`kWalkerSubsteps`) |
| Control rate | every 4 steps (50 Hz) | every 4 steps (decimation 4) = 20 ms robot time |
| Command latency | 15–30 ms | 4 physics steps = 20 ms robot time (profile `delay`) |

Because time in the four-times-larger world runs √4 = 2 times slower, 10 ms of world time
corresponds exactly to 5 ms of robot time. Kern computes decimation and delay from the world's step
rate when each duck starts:

```cpp
w->decimation = max_of(1, lround(kControlPeriod * slow * hz));   // 0.02 · 2 · 100 = 4
w->delay      = max_of(0, lround(controller.servo_delay * slow * hz));
```

If the step rate does not fit, Kern rounds to whole steps and reports the deviation ("set World
Settings > Step Rate to 100"). The delay is a queue, `pending`: in every physics step the servos
receive the target that was sent `delay` steps earlier.

`GameWorld::step` follows Glenn Fiedler's "Fix Your Timestep": the frame supplies time, the
simulation consumes it in fixed steps, the remainder stays in the accumulator, rendering
interpolates between the last two states, long frames are clamped, and simulation time is a step
counter (see [Chapter 8](08-game-runtime.md)).

The play layer decides on every second control tick (`kPlayDecisionTicks = 2`), i.e. at 25 Hz.

## 13.6 The policy set

Kern loads the same ONNX files as the robot. `velstand.onnx` is built into the engine
(`builtin_velstand_policy()`); all others are loaded at runtime by the page (Studio) or the tool and
registered with `register_walker_policy`. `WalkerPolicy` reads the ONNX format itself:

```cpp
struct WalkerPolicy {
  std::string file;                       // "velstand.onnx"
  std::vector<i32> dims;                  // 61, 512, 256, 128, 14
  std::vector<f32> mean, div;             // (obs - mean) / div
  std::vector<std::vector<f32>> weights;  // per layer [out][in] (Gemm, transB)
  std::vector<std::vector<f32>> biases;
  std::vector<std::pair<std::string, std::string>> metadata;
  void run(const f32* observation, f32* action) const;  // ELU between layers, linear at the end
};
```

`parse_onnx_policy` accepts exactly the shape of an rsl_rl actor: Sub (mean), Div (divisor), then
Gemm with Elu in between and a linear Gemm at the end, float32 tensors. It rejects everything else
with a reason ("not an rsl_rl actor", "expected Elu after a hidden Gemm", "unsupported ONNX
operator" …). The built-in velstand network carries its weights as exact float32 hex literals; the
test `micro_duck_policy_matches_the_onnx_reference` checks it against a float64 reference to within
2·10⁻⁵.

### The networks

The manifest (`third_party/microduck/manifest.json`, schema 2, `obs_len` 61, `action_len` 14,
`control_hz` 50) is built in (`micro_duck_manifest()`); `policy_set()` in `walker.cpp` reads command
encoding, duration, phase and `action_scale` from it.

| Motion | Network | Kind | Command | Key | Pad | Touch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Walking and standing | `velstand.onnx` (v8) | continuous | twist, head, body pose | W A S D | left stick | MOVE |
| Walking, older gait | `alpha_walking.onnx` + `alpha_stand.onnx` (below twist 0.05) | continuous | twist | W A S D | left stick | MOVE |
| Sit / stand up | `alpha_sitstand.onnx` | scripted | posture flag in vx (1 sit, 0 stand); 2 s sitting down, 1 s standing up | 1 | B | SIT |
| Pick up sock | `alpha_ground_pick.onnx` | episodic | phase cos/sin(2π·φ), period 4 s, ends at φ = 0.7 | 2 | Y | PICK |
| Kick left / right | `ball_kick_left.onnx` / `ball_kick_right.onnx` | episodic, 0.5 s | zero | 3 / 4 | – | KICK L / KICK R |
| Roll | `roulade.onnx` | episodic, 1 s, chain | zero; when held it keeps rolling (window 0.15 s) | 5 (hold) | X | ROLL |
| Roller skating | `roller.onnx`, action scale 0.8 | continuous | twist | W A S D | left stick | MOVE |
| Crouch on rollers | `roller_crouch.onnx`, action scale 0.8 | episodic | phase | 2 | Y | PICK |
| **Jump** (Kern) | `policies/jump.onnx` | episodic, 2 s | phase cos/sin in slots 55/56 | 7 | – | HOP |
| Beak open / closed | mouth servo (no network) | | | 6 | – | BEAK |

The gait is chosen with the **Walking network** field in the Details panel (`gait`: `velstand` or
`alpha`).

### Sequencing like robotd

`run_controller` follows `robotd/src/control.rs` in the same order:

1. **Windows first:** an expired motion ends, unwinds (`unwind_s`) or, if the chain is held,
   continues. A stand-up whose time is up is finished.
2. **Choose the network and re-encode the command:** a running motion (skill) has priority, then the
   pick, then sit/stand, then rolling, then standing (only for gait `alpha` below twist 0.05),
   otherwise walking. Each motion receives the command slots it was trained with; head and body
   slots are zeroed for skills ("zero-padded, as trained").
3. **Inference**, then scaling and low-pass filtering.
4. **Advance the windows** after the tick that used them.

`walker_skill(id, "kick_left")` starts a motion just as the daemon does. It returns `""` if the
motion starts, otherwise the reason: "a ground pick is running", "a scripted move is already
running", "the robot is sitting - stand it up first", "… is not loaded". One move at a time, and
none from sitting. All networks share the feedback of the last action (observation 34–47).

If a motion's network is not loaded yet, no other network steps in: the servos hold the home pose,
and the duck says once which file is missing.

### Action → servo target

```cpp
f32 t = clamp(home[j] + scale * action[j], -kActuatorTravel, kActuatorTravel);   // ±π, safety.rs
if (has_previous) t = alpha * t + (1 - alpha) * previous_target[j];               // low-pass
```

- Kern runs every network **as trained**: scale 1, no low-pass. Only the action scale from the
  manifest applies (rolling and crouching 0.8).
- The daemon drives the real robot with scale 0.9 and a low-pass (head 0.5, legs 0.7). That is
  sim-to-real tuning for the hardware. In the Details panel, **Actions to servo goals** (`actions`)
  chooses between `training` (default) and `robot` (daemon profile). Measured, the kicks topple over
  with 0.9 and the low-pass and stay upright with 1.0 and no filter; the duck can walk and stand with
  both (test `micro_duck_walks_with_the_robot_daemons_action_profile`).
- The target stays within the XL330's travel (±π), not within the joint range: a target beyond the
  limit pushes the joint into Box3D's hard stop, as in MuJoCo.

## 13.7 Observation and action (61 → 14)

All of Pollen's networks and Kern's jump share the same layout:

| Index | Contents |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | angular velocity of the trunk in the trunk frame (x forward, y left, z up), real rad/s |
| 3–5 | gravity in the trunk frame, upright (0, 0, −1) |
| 6–19 | joint angles − home pose, in policy order |
| 20–33 | joint velocities, one control tick old (as in training) |
| 34–47 | last action, raw |
| 48–50 | command vx, vy, ωz: ±0.4 m/s, ±0.3 m/s, ±1 rad/s; EMA 0.2 per tick as on the robot |
| 51–54 | head command (neck pitch, head pitch, yaw, roll) as a delta from the home pose; limits ±1.10 / ±1.10 / ±1.40 / ±0.31 rad |
| 55–60 | body pose; 57–59 carry z, roll, pitch. Slots 55/56 are used by Kern's jump for its phase signal |

- **Action:** 14 values, target = home + scale · action.
- **velstand network:** (obs − mean) / divisor, then 512, 256 and 128 ELU neurons, then linear 14.
- `walker_observation(id, obs)` returns, between two ticks, exactly what the controller will see on
  the next tick (test `micro_duck_observation_between_ticks_is_what_its_controller_sees`).
  `walker_last_observation` returns that of the last tick, after the command slots have been
  written.

## 13.8 Sensors

The duck sees only what the real robot sees. All sensors sit at the sites defined by the MJCF
(`head_camera`, `tof`, `imu`).

### Head ToF (VL53L8CX)

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Zones | 8 × 8 |
| Field of view | 45° × 45° |
| Range | 4 m |
| Frame rate | 15 Hz real |

Each zone is a Box3D ray cast that skips the duck's own links (`cast_ray_ignoring`). Classification
follows `kinematics/src/tof.rs`:

- **Floor** (`zone = 2`): a downward ray that covers at least 85 % of the sensor height.
- **Too close** (`zone = 1`): horizontally under 10 cm.
- **Obstacle** (`zone = 3`): everything else; in addition the nearest horizontal distance, its
  direction and the occupancy of the left and right halves (`free_left`, `free_right`, blocked under
  1 m).

`WalkerSensors` also contains the hits for the brain's obstacle model (up to 0.35 m high, up to
1.5 m away), how many rays near the walking direction reported something, the gravity, the fall
verdict and the velocity.

### Head camera (IMX219)

62° across the long side of the 16:9 image; mounted rotated by a quarter turn. When upright the
image is portrait, 62° high and 37.4° wide. `walker_camera(id, cam)` returns the camera;
`walker_sees(id, target, visible, bearing, distance)` checks whether the centre of an actor lies in
the image and whether the first hit of a ray towards it is the actor itself. While playing, the
Studio shows the camera image in the top right of the viewport (show flag **Robot Camera**), with
the ToF grid next to it.

When the duck looks down (neck −1.1, head 1.1), the camera points 87° downwards and sees 6–15 cm in
front of the trunk (test `micro_duck_camera_sees_the_floor_in_front_when_looking_down`).

### IMU and falling

Gravity in the trunk frame. "Fallen" means the same as in the daemon: gz > −0.5 for 200 ms
(`fall_gravity_z`, `fall_debounce_ms`). A brief tilt while stumbling does not count. When an
autonomous duck has fallen, `walker_state` reports "GetUp"; velstand v8 gets back up by itself.

### Mouth encoder

`walker_holding(id)`: if the mouth servo is set to "closed" but is still more than 2° open,
something is in the beak. Closed on nothing, the beak rests at 0.0° against its pads; a sock holds
it open at 3.6–7.8°. The mouth travels from −5° (pressed against the pads) to 30°.

## 13.9 Who is driving

On every control tick `drive_walkers` decides whose command applies, in this order:

1. **Hand:** the player's stick (over 5 % deflection) or a robot key sets `manual_left` to 2 s. While
   it runs, the hand rules, and autonomy and play layer pause.
2. **Logic:** a command from **Walk** or **Look** (`walker_command`, `walker_look`), for the given
   duration or until the next command.
3. **Autonomy:** the brain (`think_walker`) and, above it, the play layer (`play_walker`), when the
   **Autonomous** field (`autonomous`) is on.

Twist, head and body pose are then limited and smoothed with an EMA (twist 0.2, head at the rate of
the sender). When the duck is standing still (no twist, no motion, upright) and nobody has steered
the head for 2 s, the **idle gaze** takes over (`idle_walker`, port of `robotd/src/idle_head.rs`):
glances along minimum-jerk curves, small saccades, slow drift, dozing off over time and an
occasional yawn.

### In the hand: robotd's pick-up detection

Like robotd (`[pickup]`, on by default since the v2 model; duck-control and robotd `pickup.rs`), the
duck notices when a hand lifts it, and then does not keep stepping in the air. Pollen's classifier
`pickup_detector.onnx` (a temporal CNN with about 8,000 parameters, built in as bytes in
`third_party/microduck/pickup_detector.inc`) reads the last second on every control tick: 50 rows of
63 features (`kPickupWindow`, `kPickupFeatures`) – gyro, gravity, joints minus home, joint
velocities, the previous tick's target minus home, servo currents and a pause flag. It answers with
p(held). Kern evaluates the network itself and deterministically (`parse_onnx_pickup`: normalise and
mask, a Gather of the columns it reads, three Conv1d with ELU, then Gemm, ELU, Gemm, Sigmoid).

| Step | Rule (`drive_walkers`, after duck-control `pickup.rs`) |
|---|---|
| Pause | p > 0.8 on 5 ticks in a row |
| Held | no network runs; the targets ramp in 300 ms from the last target to `PAUSE_POSE` (the walking policy's average stance); `walker_driving` reports `held`, `walker_state` "Held" |
| Resume | p < 0.35 on 4 ticks in a row, after at least 300 ms of pause; the networks start fresh (last action 0, low-pass without an anchor), as robotd does after a pause |

- Only a full window is evaluated; a padded one would be out of the training distribution.
- It only watches while the walking or standing network has the duck. A motion, sitting, roller
  skates (the model has never seen wheels) or the Lehrer's learner end a pause and clear the window.
- Currents: Kern has no servo currents. The shipped model does not read these columns (the graph
  drops them with a Gather); Kern fills in 0.
- In the Studio you lift the duck by its trunk in **Simulate** (or while watching with the observer
  camera, as in the example level). This grab holds the trunk in its
  orientation as a hand does, instead of letting it swing on a point (`MouseGrab::begin` with
  `hold_rotation`, [Chapter 6](06-physics.md)).

*Measured:* test `micro_duck_pickup_detector_matches_onnxruntime`: on Pollen's two fixture windows,
Kern's evaluation differs from onnxruntime by less than 10⁻⁵. Test
`micro_duck_pauses_in_the_hand_and_walks_when_put_down`: 20 s autonomous without a false pause;
lifted 15 cm (real), it pauses and stays paused; set back down, it walks on and does not fall.

## 13.10 The brain: autonomy

When nobody drives the duck, it decides for itself (`src/game/walker_brain.cpp`). The source is
Pollen's daemon `autod` (branch `autonomous-mode`, commit `df9a750` from 01.10.2026, Apache-2.0),
with its states, durations, transition weights and numbers. The states issue the same commands as a
gamepad: twist, head pose with its own smoothing rate, body pose, mouth and sounds.

| State | What the duck does |
|---|---|
| Chill | stands, small head drifts, breathing beak (3–7 s) |
| LookAround | big glances, head tilted (3.5–7 s) |
| Wander | walks to a target 1–3 m away in one of 16 directions, preferring rarely visited ones; ends at 0.25 m or with no 10 cm of progress in 8 s (at most 90 s) |
| TurnInPlace | turns at 80–100 % of 1.5 rad/s, head leading |
| Zoomies | full speed, jerky turns, "Wheee" (only with fresh ToF and more than 1.2 m clear) |
| Stretch | stretches, yawns, coos, shakes its head |
| Ruffle | feather shake at 4.5 Hz |
| Dance | groove with the body pose |
| Sneeze | breathe in, hesitate, sneeze, look around dazed |
| GroundPick | the pick-up motion, followed by a quack |
| Nap | sits down and dozes or rests (12–40 s) |
| Petted | being petted (`walker_pet`) |
| BallPlay | sees a ball, walks up and lines it up in front of its left foot (rebuilt from Pollen's original runtime, below) |
| Kick | Pollen's left kick, followed by a quack burst and a look around |

As in the original, on top of this come an **energy** (0–1) that weights the next state, a
**novelty grid** (0.5 m cells), **quack bursts**, **hearing** (`walker_hear`: a voice gets an
answer, a noise briefly wakes it) and the **obstacle model** from `obstacle.rs`: confirmed ToF hits
in the corridor ahead (half-width 0.12 m, measured from the beak 0.18 m in front of the trunk);
below 0.45 m it brakes and swerves, below 0.15 m it turns away and blocks the spot for 5 min.

Odometry is the trunk pose. Kern has no microphone and no touch sensor; hearing and petting come in
through the API. Randomness comes from a generator seeded with the actor, so autonomy is
deterministic too. While a motion is running, the duck is sitting down or standing up, or has
fallen, the brain stays quiet.

### BallPlay: from Pollen's original runtime, on by default

Pollen's runtime (`apirrone/microduck_runtime`, `autonomous.rs`) had a state BallPlay: "approach /
line up / kick". `autod` left it out only because the daemon has no ball detector yet
(`docs/design/autonomous.md` §4). The runtime's code is not public. Kern therefore rebuilds BallPlay
from that description, with Pollen's own kick network (`ball_kick_left.onnx`) and the ball position
from its training. BallPlay is **on by default**: it belongs to Pollen's software in Kern, not to
Kern extras.

It runs when the duck is autonomous, wears no roller skates, `kick_left` is loaded, the play layer
is not available (Section 13.11), and the brain is not currently kicking, napping, being petted,
picking or sitting.

- **Seeing:** a ball (a dynamic sphere actor with tag `ball` or the name "Ball", without a parent, on
  the floor, i.e. at most 8 cm high) in the head camera's image (`walker_sees`: within the field of
  view of the IMX219 optics and not occluded) starts BallPlay. If it sees several, it takes the
  nearest and keeps to it.
- **Approach:** with the head lowered (only the head pitches, not the neck: bowing shifts the weight
  forward and the gait suffers), slower the closer it gets, down to 35 cm. If the ball is more than
  0.6 rad to the side, it first turns on the spot. There is no obstacle gate here: what the ToF sees
  ahead is the ball.
- **Line up:** in small steps (0.2–0.25 m/s, never below what the gait starts walking at from a
  stand), the head on the ball (−1.1 rad), until the ball lies where the kick training puts it
  (`microduck_ball_kick_env_cfg.py`: 9 cm ahead of, 4.2 cm beside the left foot, ±1.5 cm, measured
  from the foot). If it loses the ball from view close in front of it, it steps back with the head
  lowered until it sees it again; it does not kick where the ball was.
- **Kick:** head at home (the kick was trained that way), 3 s of calm until it is slower than
  1 cm/s, then the kick. Afterwards a quack burst, and this ball is of no interest for 2–5 s.
- **Giving up:** a ball it has not seen for 6 s is given up while it is far away (closer than 0.4 m
  it goes by the last position). After 45 s without a kick it leaves the ball alone for 20 s. In both
  cases LookAround follows.

Deviations from the original, as far as known: the detector is a geometric visibility test instead
of a neural detector in the camera image. It only kicks with the left foot, because the right kick
tips the duck over in Kern (Section 13.15). The approach and line-up values are Kern's, because the
runtime's values cannot be read.

**Play layer instead of BallPlay:** with Kern extras on and `play.onnx` loaded
(`walker_play_available`), the brain starts no BallPlay; then Kern's learned network plays with ball
and sock (Section 13.11). While the play layer is engaged, the brain starts neither picking nor
sitting.

## 13.11 The play layer (`walker_play.cpp`)

Pollen's networks are the duck's motions; none of them can see. What the duck does with a ball or a
sock – look at it, walk to it, kick it, pick it up, carry it off, spit it out – is decided by a
network of its own: `policies/play.onnx`. It is Kern's own (only with Kern extras) and then takes
over instead of Pollen's BallPlay (Section 13.10); Kern's former hand-written code for Fetch and
Carry has been removed. The play layer itself contains no hand-written play logic; around the
network there is only perception.

![The duck at play (duck shown in this book's own design)](../buch/bilder/duck-play.png)

### Where it sits

The play layer sits where a gamepad would. On every second control tick (25 Hz) it reads what the
camera's detector reports and what the duck feels, and answers with a walking command, head, beak
and which motion it wants to start. It then replaces the brain's commands (`auto_cmd`, `auto_head`,
`auto_mouth`), and `walker_state` reports "Play".

It is available when

- **Kern extras** is on (`kernExtras`, Details › Controller › "Kern extras (ball play, sock
  fetching, beak in the pick)"),
- the duck is not wearing roller skates and has a mouth servo,
- `play.onnx` is loaded (or the Lehrer drives it externally),
- **Autonomous** is on: the play layer's walking command, head and beak go through the autonomy
  channel and only take effect then.

It is **engaged** as long as it knows an object, has something in its beak or a motion it started
itself is running, and nobody is driving by hand or via logic:

```cpp
const bool driven = w.manual_left > 0 || w.drive_left != 0;
p.engaged = p.external || (!driven && (p.track[0].known || p.track[1].known || holding || p.started_move));
```

Otherwise Pollen's brain runs as before. **Manual control pauses the play layer:** a stick, a robot
key or a logic command switches it off until the 2 s of hand time have elapsed or the logic command
is over.

### The detector: a device model

Like the servo, the detector (`sense_play`) is a device model with parameters that the Lehrer
varies:

- For each class (ball, sock) it looks for the **nearest object** that lies in the camera image and
  is not occluded. A ball is a dynamic sphere actor with tag `ball` or the name "Ball"; a sock is an
  actor with tag `sock` (seen when any of its parts is visible).
- Its position is the centre of the object **on the floor**: a ball higher than 8 cm or a sock
  higher than 3 cm (real) does not count – a sock in the beak, for example.
- **Noise:** standard deviation = `sigma_abs` + `sigma_rel` · distance (monocular estimate; the
  error grows with distance).
- **Dropouts:** with probability `dropout` a frame reports nothing.

```cpp
struct DetectorModel { f32 sigma_abs = 0, sigma_rel = 0, dropout = 0; u64 seed = 1; };
world.set_walker_detector(duck, {0.002f, 0.01f, 0.1f, 42});
```

In the Studio the detector is noise-free; the Lehrer draws its values per episode
([Chapter 14](14-lehrer.md#148-learning-the-play-layer)). The noise comes from a separate,
deterministic generator.

### The tracker

- The tracker keeps the last estimate when the object leaves the image, together with its **age**.
  After 3 s without a report it forgets it; for an object closer than 0.35 m (below the camera; it
  does not move by itself) only after 30 s.
- **Habituation:** it does not report a ball that was just kicked for 15 s, nor a sock that was
  carried off and put down for 40 s. A sock counts as put down if it was carried at least 0.25 m; if
  it was lost while being lifted, it remains a target.
- **Carrying:** from the moment of grabbing, it counts how far and how long the duck has been
  carrying something.

### Observation (`kPlayObs` = 23)

All positions in real metres in the duck's **heading frame** (trunk direction, yaw only): x ahead,
y left. No true positions.

| Index | Name (`observation_names`) | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `ball_known` | 1 if the tracker knows a ball |
| 1, 2 | `ball_ahead`, `ball_left` | estimated ball position |
| 3 | `ball_age` | seconds since the last report (up to 3) |
| 4 | `sock_known` | 1 if the tracker knows a sock |
| 5, 6 | `sock_ahead`, `sock_left` | estimated sock position |
| 7 | `sock_age` | seconds since the last report (up to 3) |
| 8, 9 | `left_foot_ahead`, `left_foot_left` | left ankle relative to the trunk |
| 10 | `holding` | mouth encoder: something in the beak |
| 11 | `beak_open` | beak angle, 0 = closed (−5°), 1 = open (30°), limited to −0.5 … 1.5 |
| 12 | `walking` | 1 if the walking or standing network is driving |
| 13 | `picking` | 1 while the pick is running |
| 14 | `kicking` | 1 while `kick_left` is running |
| 15 | `pick_phase` | phase of the pick (0 … 0.7) |
| 16–18 | `gravity_x/y/z` | gravity in the trunk frame |
| 19 | `carried_m` | distance carried so far (up to 2 m), only while holding |
| 20 | `carried_s` | time carried so far (up to 10 s), only while holding |
| 21 | `still_s` | how long it has been standing still (up to 4 s) |
| 22 | `walking_s` | how long it has been walking forward (+) or backward (−) (±4 s) |

**Standing still** has been redefined as what the legs are doing, not what was commanded: both soles
touch something that is not the duck itself, the play layer is engaged, and no motion is running.
The reason is given in the code: Pollen's gait may not start at all on "a little forward" – that is
standing, wherever the threshold lies in this physics or on the real duck.

`walker_play_observation(id, out)` returns the observation without side effects and exactly between
two ticks.

### Actions (`kPlayActions` = 9)

| Index | Name (`action_names`) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `forward` | vx = value · 0.3 m/s |
| 1 | `strafe` | vy = value · 0.2 m/s |
| 2 | `turn` | ωz = value · 1.0 rad/s |
| 3 | `head_yaw` | head yaw, limited to ±1, · 1.1 rad |
| 4 | `head_pitch_down` | head pitch, limited to −0.5 … 1, · 1.1 rad (+ looks down) |
| 5 | `beak_open` | > 0: beak open |
| 6 | `kick_left` | > 0.5: start Pollen's left kick |
| 7 | `ground_pick` | > 0.5: start Pollen's pick |
| 8 | `neck_bow` | bend the neck: max(0, value), limited to 1, · 1.1 rad downwards |

- All values are first limited to ±1.5; non-finite values become 0.
- The three walking commands have a **dead zone like a gamepad stick**: below 20 % of the range
  (0.06 m/s, 0.04 m/s, 0.2 rad/s) the duck stands. A network that answers 0.1 instead of 0 thus
  stays standing.
- **Backwards** the play layer starts the gait as Pollen's `autod` does (`walkable` in
  `walker_brain.cpp`): the first 0.6 s at full speed (0.3 m/s), then never slower than 0.15 m/s.
  From a stand, a slower step back does not step at all. Forwards the network sets the pace itself;
  it has learned to give the gait a firm push from a stand.
- The head follows with smoothing rate 0.3; body pose and "Wheee" are off.
- A motion only starts if none is running and the beak is empty; otherwise the daemon refuses. When
  a kick starts, the tracker ignores the ball for 15 s.
- During a pick the beak belongs to the network: it has learned when to open and when to bite.
  Without the play layer, Kern extras opens the beak at phase 0.16 and bites at 0.24.

### Sock and ball

**Sock** (`micro_duck_sock_actors()`): seven cloth lumps 12.5 mm in diameter, loosely heaped 3.4 cm
high (four on the floor, two on top of them, one on top), 25 g in total, each joined to its
neighbours by soft springs (Box3D distance joints, 15 Hz, damping 0.7). It gives way under the beak,
and whatever the beak grabs drags the rest along. Each lump has surface `cloth` and rolling
resistance 0.5: round cloth lumps would otherwise roll, and the heap would creep across the floor.

```cpp
std::vector<game::Actor> sock = game::micro_duck_sock_actors();  // index 0 is the root "Sock"
// remap ids, parents and joint targets as in add_micro_duck, then level.add(...)
```

**Ball:** a dynamic sphere actor with tag `ball` (or name "Ball"). In the example level, a 12 cm
toy ball of 50 g with rolling resistance 0.03 (rubber on wood); without rolling resistance it would
roll forever.

### What happens in the example level

The Micro Duck level (`game::micro_duck_level()`) is a 2.5 × 2.5 m (real) room with a wooden floor
(friction 1, surface `floor`), a shoebox, a stack of books, a crate, a flowerpot, a ball and two
socks. The duck is autonomous and has Kern extras on. A level blueprint demonstrates the Robots
nodes: it makes the duck quack once when the ball comes into the camera image. This is a
demonstration of the blueprint, not behaviour of the duck.

### Results

Measured over 32 episodes per row (`docs/lehrer.md`, section 8; details in
[Chapter 14](14-lehrer.md)):

| | ball kicked away | sock picked up | picked up, carried off, spat out | grips that held | falls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| network, clean robot | 9 of 11 (17 s on average) | 4 of 21 | 1 of 21 | 2 of 14 | 2 of 32 |
| network, full variation | 7 of 11 | 13 of 21 | 0 of 21 | 1 of 15 | 10 of 32 |

The runtime tests `micro_duck_plays_with_a_ball_on_its_own` (ball 30 cm in front of the duck: kicked,
rolls more than 25 cm away, no fall) and `micro_duck_goes_for_a_sock_and_bites` (sock 30 cm in front:
walk up, stand, pick, bite) check in Studio physics that only the network makes the decisions.

## 13.12 Controls in the Studio

![Touch controls on a phone (shown for Orb Run; the duck shows its own buttons)](../buch/bilder/phone-play-390.png)

The inputs end up in `PlayInput::robot`, a bit field of the robot keys being held:

| Bit | Motion | Keyboard | Gamepad | Touch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sit / stand up | 1 | B | SIT |
| 2 | Pick (on rollers: crouch) | 2 | Y | PICK |
| 4 | Kick left | 3 | – | KICK L |
| 8 | Kick right | 4 | – | KICK R |
| 16 | Roll (held: keep going) | 5 | X | ROLL |
| 32 | Beak open/closed | 6 | – | BEAK |

| 128 | "Wheee" (held) | V | LT | WHEEE |
| 256 | **Jump** (Kern) | **7** | – | **HOP** |

Walking: W/S or the left stick forward/backward (up to 0.4 m/s), A/D or the stick sideways to turn
(up to 1 rad/s). On the gamepad a radial dead zone of 0.10 with a power curve of 1.28 applies. On the
phone there is the MOVE stick and the buttons at the top; QUACK and PICK are in the bottom row under
the thumb. Every robot key and every stick deflection sets the hand time to 2 s and pauses autonomy
and the play layer.

The voice follows `robotd/src/sound.rs`: "greet" when the duck comes to life and when the gamepad
returns, "inquire" when it is gone, "chirp" on a fresh press of quack, "wheee" while held, "peck"
when sitting down. The voice is a bit-exact port of Pollen's synthesiser
(`src/audio/duck_voice.cpp`, [Chapter 7](07-audio.md)), with a voice of its own for each duck.

### The jump

Key **7**, touch **HOP** or the logic node **Robot Move** with "jump" start Kern's jump. It runs like
Pollen's kicks: a hard switch to `jump.onnx`, and back after 2 s (`kJumpSeconds`). The network sees
the phase t / 2 s as cos and sin in observation slots 55 and 56 (`kJumpPhaseSlot`), which Pollen's
networks never use; the rest of the command is 0. In the Studio (test
`micro_duck_jumps_for_real_in_the_studio`): 3 out of 3 real jumps, rise 9.0 mm, flight 120 ms,
tilt 8°, after which the duck stands and walks again. How the network was created is described in
[Chapter 14](14-lehrer.md#149-jump-results).

### Loading networks

The Studio (`web/studio/game.mjs`) asks the engine for missing networks
(`kern_studio_pending_policies`) and loads them: Pollen's networks from `microduck/`, Kern's own
(`jump.onnx`, `play.onnx`) from `policies/` – on the developer page from `third_party/microduck/` and
`kern/policies/`. A file that fails to load is requested again after 5, 10, 20 s … (at most once a
minute). Networks that the Lehrer has stored in the browser on the same device (IndexedDB
"kern-lehrer") are loaded by the Studio as well, and open Studio tabs pick up a new one immediately
(BroadcastChannel "kern-lehrer").

## 13.13 The API in C++

```cpp
game::GameWorld& w = ...;                       // running world with a duck
const game::ActorId duck = w.walkers().front();

w.walker_command(duck, 0.2f, 0, 0, 3.0f);       // 0.2 m/s forward, for 3 s
w.walker_look(duck, 0.5f, 0.2f, 2.0f);           // head yaw / pitch
std::string why = w.walker_skill(duck, "pick");  // "" = started
bool holds = w.walker_holding(duck);

game::GameWorld::WalkerSensors s;
w.walker_sensors(duck, s);                       // ToF, gravity, fall
bool visible; f32 bearing, distance;
w.walker_sees(duck, ball, visible, bearing, distance);

std::string net = w.walker_driving(duck);        // "walk", "stand", "ground_pick", "kick_left", "jump", "held", ...
std::string state = w.walker_state(duck);        // "Wander", "BallPlay", "Play", "Held", "Driven", "Logic", "GetUp", ...
```

For the Lehrer there are additionally `set_walker_external` / `set_walker_action` (a learning policy
instead of the networks), `walker_tick_due`, `walker_probe` (measurements for reward and critic in
real units), `walker_push` (a push) and, for the play layer, `set_walker_play_external`,
`set_walker_play_action`, `walker_play_due` and `set_walker_detector`. They are explained in
[Chapter 14](14-lehrer.md).

## 13.15 What is missing, and why

- **Gear backlash ±1°:** not modelled. In training it is a randomisation, and the policy is robust
  to it. A candidate for matching ([Chapter 16](16-calibration.md)).
- **Armature:** as a disc that turns with the link, not as a geared rotor (Section 13.3).
- **Friction mixing:** Box3D mixes two surfaces geometrically, MuJoCo takes the sole's. On a floor
  with 0.6 this would give 0.77, within the training variation of 0.7–1.3; in the example level the
  floor is 1.0 and the sole grips with 1.0.
- **Kick right:** in Kern it sometimes tips over after the kick. The cause is open (backlash, contact
  softness, foot friction).
- **BallPlay:** rebuilt from the description of the runtime, whose code is not public; the detector
  is a geometric visibility test, only the left kick (Section 13.10).
- **Servo currents:** Kern has none. The pick-up detector gets 0; the shipped model does not read
  these columns (Section 13.9).
- **Kern's own, not in the original:** the play layer, the beak timing during the pick (both only
  with Kern extras) and the jump. Always on, because they are pure sound: plastic and footstep
  sounds, and the servo noise derived from the load.

The complete table of differences from Pollen's runtime is in `docs/walker.md`, section 13.
